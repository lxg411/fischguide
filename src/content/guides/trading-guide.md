Trading is where Fisch's real economy lives — limited boats, rod skins and mutated fish
move for millions of S$. But unlike most Roblox trading games, Fisch has **no single
value list everyone agrees on**, and that one fact explains most bad trades. This guide
covers the three value systems, how to sanity-check an offer, and the scams to watch for.

## The three value systems (and why numbers disagree)

When someone in trade chat says "that's worth 5M," the first question is: *on whose list?*

- **Proto spreadsheet** — the oldest community list, point-based rather than S$-based.
  Items are quoted in Proto points (a top boat might be "2,100 Proto").
- **TrueVal** — a Discord-run list quoted in S$, maintained by a dedicated value team.
  Tends to quote higher on collector items.
- **Trade Hub** — market-observation data quoted in S$, based on what trades actually
  close at. Tends to sit below TrueVal on hyped items.

The same item routinely differs 30%+ between systems — Evangeline has been quoted at
S$ 10.8M on TrueVal and S$ 7.2M on Trade Hub in the same week. Neither is "wrong";
they measure different things (asking consensus vs closed trades). Our
[value list](/values/) shows the spread as a range so you can see disagreement at a glance.

**Practical rule:** before any big trade, agree with your partner *which system you're
quoting*. Most fair-trade disputes are really system disputes.

## Reading a WFL (Win / Fair / Lose)

A WFL is just both sides of the trade valued on the same system:

1. Value your side and their side **on one system** (don't mix Proto and TrueVal).
2. Mutated fish = base price × mutation multiplier — check the
   [multiplier table](/mutations/) rather than trusting the other trader's math.
3. Demand matters as much as the number. A "fair" trade into a low-demand item is a
   soft loss — you'll struggle to move it later. Our list carries demand ratings for
   exactly this reason.
4. Anything flagged *unstable*, *hoarded* or *manipulated* deserves a second check the
   day of the trade. Those tags mean the number moved recently or someone is pushing it.

## Scam patterns that actually show up

- **System-switching** — they quote their item on TrueVal (high) and yours on Trade Hub
  (low). Counter: one system for both sides, agreed up front.
- **Multiplier inflation** — claiming a mutation is rarer or higher-multiplier than it
  is ("this is 15×, trust me"). The table maxes at Aether 12×. Check, don't trust.
- **Duped-fish laundering** — Glitched fish carry a 0× multiplier for a reason. A
  "cheap" Glitched trophy fish is worth exactly nothing at sale.
- **Rush pressure** — "offer expires in 60 seconds" is the oldest tell in trading. Real
  collectors let you check a list first.
- **Off-platform "middlemen"** — anyone routing you to Discord DMs for an "official
  middleman service" is fishing for your account, not your fish.

## Building value as a newer trader

You don't need a Cthulu's Revenge to start. The mid-tier bracket (S$ 1.4M–2.5M) is the
most liquid part of the market — items move fast, spreads are thin, and mistakes are
survivable. Flip there while you learn how demand behaves, and treat the
[rod tier list](/rod-tier-list/) as your earning engine: a high-luck rod catching
high-multiplier mutations is steadier income than any single lucky flip.
